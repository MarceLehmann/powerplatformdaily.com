faq:
  - question: "Which Microsoft components does the AI-enabled donor management reference architecture combine?"
    answer: "The reference architecture combines Microsoft 365, Power Platform, Copilot Studio, Dataverse and AI Builder. AI Builder reads donation forms, Dataverse holds the donor information as the central source of truth, Power Automate runs the workflows and system integrations, and Copilot Studio agents answer donor enquiries and suggest next actions. Each component covers one step, from form intake to donor follow-up."
  - question: "What manual donation processing problem does an AI-based donor workflow address?"
    answer: "It addresses the manual handling of donation forms. In the described situation, forms arrive by email or post, staff type the data into spreadsheets, compare donor details with financial systems and follow up through disconnected applications. One organisation received hundreds of forms every week, so data entry and checking absorbed time that could have gone into donor engagement."
  - question: "Do not-for-profits adopt AI donor management mainly to cut headcount?"
    answer: "No. The author says most organisations he works with are not looking to reduce headcount but to increase capacity. The benefit he stresses is staff moving from paperwork to relationship building and donor engagement. That can improve donor retention, and an organisation becomes more effective when staff spend less time processing paperwork and more time engaging with donors."
  - question: "Why should donor information be centralised before building Copilot Studio agents?"
    answer: "Because an agent can only be as good as the information it can reach. Many organisations spread knowledge across Outlook mailboxes, SharePoint sites, network drives, PDFs, spreadsheets and legacy applications. The author states that AI output quality is determined by the quality of the available information, so it has to be properly organised, centralised and accessible. In the reference architecture, Dataverse is that central source of truth."
  - question: "Will staff and donors notice the AI in future donor management?"
    answer: "Probably less and less. The author expects AI to become largely invisible to end users, who will simply notice faster responses, more personalised engagement, less administration and easier access to information. The stated aim is that organisations spend less time administering donations and more time building relationships with the people who choose to support their mission."

## TL;DR

Microsoft&\#39;s AI-enabled donor management reference architecture uses AI Builder to extract donation form data, Dataverse as a central source of truth, Power Automate for workflows and integrations, and Copilot Studio agents for donor enquiries, triage and next-best-action recommendations.

## Why it matters

- Makers: Use AI-powered document extraction for donation forms, store validated data in Dataverse, and automate hand-offs with Power Automate to reduce manual entry.
- Admins/IT: Organise and centralise information before building agents, because fragmented content limits what Copilot can access and recommend.
- Leadership/Business: Measure the solution by increased capacity for donor engagement, fundraising and services, not only by administrative cost reduction.
